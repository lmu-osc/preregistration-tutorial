Evaluating preregistrations

Recap: We practiced improving individual elements of preregistration.

Now we will turn our attention to This critical evaluation exercise is helpful for you to improve your understanding of doing a preregistration by examining real examples. This exercise provides guidance and resources in how to critically evaluate existing study preregistrations. By increasing familiarity with the content of study preregistrations across a wide range of research areas, you are encouraged to think and relate these concepts to your own research projects.

Learning objective: To be able to evaluate the quality of study preregistrations.

Instructions:

  • Pick one preregistration from the “Experimental studies” category and one more from any other study category to evaluate two contrasting examples of completed preregistrations.

  • Read the two preregistrations and use the guiding questions below to help you assess them.

  • After evaluating the preregistrations, consider the reflection questions.

This exercise should take about 50 minutes to complete.

Note

Even if the examples use different research methods to your own it is still useful to compare. This will help broaden your perspectives in what preregistered studies can look like across research domains.

TipGuiding questions

  1. What do you notice about the level of detail in the research questions and/or hypotheses?

  2. How do experimental studies compare to other study types in how they handle missing data?

  3. Was there additional information you weren’t aware formed part of the preregistration process?

  4. Do any of the preregistrations provide information about exploratory analyses?

  5. What aspects of the preregistrations worked well? Which could be improved?

Comparing preregistrations with final published studies

Extension exercise

This extension exercise helps you to compare the content of study preregistrations to the final publications.

Instructions:

  • Pick one of the three studies below

  • Next briefly read through the preregistration/registered report

  • Then take a look at the final peer-reviewed publication and answer the guiding questions

  • This extension exercise should take about 45 minutes.

TipGuiding questions

  1. Did the authors preregister their study before or after data collection?

  2. Are all statistical tests mentioned in the preregistration/registered report reported in the publication?

  3. What are two similarities between the content of the preregistration/registered report and the final published study?

  4. Where in the final publication do authors report their preregistration/registered report?

  5. Are there any deviations from the study preregistration/registered report mentioned in the final publication?
