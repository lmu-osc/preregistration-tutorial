Final recap, practical tips and resources

Final recap:

link to Max’s dilemma

This page contains a collection of common misconceptions, frequently asked questions and concerns about preregistration. This page is designed to be used as a reference guide. This collection is organized according to the stages of doing a preregistration. The colored tags indicate the topic the question falls under.

The topics include: Terms and concepts, Study type, Practical, Ways of working, Statistics

Thinking about doing a preregistration

The data I am working with is descriptive, is a study preregistration still useful?

TipSuggestion

…

What is the difference between a preregistration and a registered report?

Terms and concepts

TipSuggestion

Registered reports are a publishing format where a study undergoes two stages of peer review:

  • stage 1: introduction and methods in advance of data collection/analysis and

  • stage 2: which includes the full study findings including results/discussion (parsons2022?).

Registered reports have been described in the literature as “Two-stage review with in-principle acceptance” (mayo-wilson2025?).

Is it true that doing a preregistration increases the risk of being scooped?

Practical Ways of working

TipSuggestion

The fear of scooping is understandable given the current competitive research culture of “publish or perish”. Preregistrations do not need to be made public right away and can be embargoed for a period of years (e.g. Open Science Framework preregistrations can be embargoed for 4 years from first submission). The embargo period allows you time after preregistering your study to conduct the research, analyze, write up and publish the findings.

Preparing a preregistration

I am keen to try out doing a preregistration but my supervisor thinks it is a “waste of time”, what should I do?

Ways of working

TipSuggestion

Often a reluctance to try out preregistration comes from a place of lack of familiarity with the process. Sharing resources, and suggest a group meeting to discuss/learn more about preregistration to increase familiarity. Reframe preregistration as a creative opportunity to plan great science! Planning is where the science is, as other research processes, like data collection can be delegated.

Relate to this situation? See the preregistration case study which provides a number of strategies to address this kind of barrier.

How do I know my planned statistical analysis is the right one?

Statistics

TipSuggestion

To get a better understanding of your real data before your collect it you can run computer simulations and power analyses. Briefly this involves:

  • Simulating random data with the distribution and sample size of expected real data and run the statistical test planned for the study.

  • Repeating the statistical test (e.g. 100 x) and check results are within the false-positive finding rate of 5%.

  • Runing a power analysis by simulating data with the expected effect size for the study and check model registers effect at least 80% of the time.

Take a look at our simulation tutorial here or a more beginner friendly guide here.

Submitting a preregistration

I forgot to report a statistical test, can I modify my preregistration?

Practical

TipSuggestion

Mistakes happen! If you spot the mistake before you carry out your analysis, you can report a “deviation from preregistration” in a new document where you transparently describe the change, rationale and effect of change to the study results (see guidance here).

I accidentally included sensitive information in my preregistration, can I withdraw it?

Practical

TipSuggestion

There are several options depending on when you decide to withdraw your preregistration:

Depending on the preregistration platform you use, there is an author approval window after creation of preregistration (2 days for OSF, 6 days for AsPredicted) where you can cancel it.

If you decide to withdraw after the preregistration is finalized, you can create a new preregistration with the sensitive information omitted and document the rationale for withdrawing the original preregistration. More information about the withdrawal process on OSF

Post preregistration: reporting research

What sort of things from my preregistration do I need to report in my manuscript?

Practical Statistics Study type

TipSuggestion

You will need to reference the preregistrated work in the methods section of your manuscript by providing a link to the repository. All the main components of a preregistration (e.g. hypotheses/research questions, sampling plan, variable description, design plan, outliers and exceptions, analyses) and any deviations from the preregistration. You can use this OSF checklist to help you remember what should be reported in your manuscript.

Where can I find information on which journals accept preregistrations/registered reports?

Practical

TipSuggestion

There are over 300 journals that adopt the registered report format (see list of journals here).

Practical tips

As we come to the end of this tutorial, you should feel more knowledgeable and confident about the Open Science practice of preregistration and how to carry out one in your own research. Even if you are not quite ready to implement a study preregistration just yet, you should have the tools to critically evaluate your own research planning approaches and existing preregistered studies.
